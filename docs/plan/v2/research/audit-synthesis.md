# harness-creator: post-audit decision briefing

## 1. Verdict

harness-creator is already aligned with Claude-5-generation context engineering at the level of doctrine, and misaligned at the level of *coverage and truthfulness*. Three of the article's seven shifts are not merely satisfied but stated more sharply in the skill than in the article — conviction-over-compliance (SKILL.md:86, skills.md:5-14), progressive disclosure as an optimum with a named silent failure mode (skills.md:22-28), and the CLAUDE.md/Skills content split (claude-md-and-rules.md:25, skills.md:44). Across 44 verified findings, not one wrong *doctrine* claim survived, and of 14 high-stakes product mechanics re-checked against live docs, 14 were confirmed verbatim. The real problems are elsewhere and they are concrete: the skill makes **five false claims about its own scripts** (SKILL.md:111 on pointer checking, SKILL.md:59 on drift, interview.md:99/:103/:132 on spec parsing), its linter **emits a blocking error on a correct CLAUDE.md that mentions a maintainer's email address**, and it has **zero awareness of auto memory** — a second always-loaded instruction surface holding the exact content categories it routes to CLAUDE.md. Rightsizing is the *smallest* of the opportunities: after protecting the 6,521-word do-not-cut set and exempting hooks.md per C-2, the honest compression available is roughly 930 words against roughly 780 words of required additions. So: **already ~80% aligned; the four things that matter are (1) the script-truth defects, (2) the linter bugs that fail correct harnesses, (3) auto memory and the personal-vs-team scope axis, (4) domain narrative in the worked examples.** The one genuinely large context win — cutting the always-loaded surface from 4,833 words to ~1,970 — is not a trimming exercise at all; it is a structural decision about whether interview.md keeps loading unconditionally, and it is Tradeoff #1 below.

## 1c. The bug list

Ranked by whether a correct harness fails, then by silence of the failure.

**B1 — `_AT_IMPORT_RE` fails a correct harness, at Error level.** `validate_harness.py:38` is `(?<!\`)@([\w./\-]+\.\w+)(?!\`)`. I executed it: `contact ops@acme.com` → `acme.com`; `stay on react@18.2.0` → `18.2.0`. Each becomes `E: @acme.com import target does not exist` at validate_harness.py:419, and validate_harness.py:486 turns any E into a non-zero exit — so **Hard line 2 (SKILL.md:112, "not finished until `validate_harness.py` exits 0") is unsatisfiable for a CLAUDE.md that names a maintainer email or a pinned version.** Two further defects in the same regex: `See @README for overview` (the memory doc's own example of a real import) is missed entirely, and a `@docs/nope.md` inside a fenced code block still matches, contrary to the documented "Import parsing skips Markdown code spans and fenced code blocks." Fix: make the fence/code-span skip real, drop the mandatory dot-extension, and require a path-shaped target.

**B2 — Wrap-up validates before the edits it claims to catch.** SKILL.md:52 runs `validate_harness.py` first; :56 and :57 then mutate the spec and CLAUDE.md; :60 proposes a commit. SKILL.md:54-55 justifies the run by "the two bullets below can themselves introduce new drift," and :59 asserts "the validate_harness.py run just above will independently catch any spec-vs-disk drift this introduces" — about a run that already finished. **B3 — CLAUDE.md discovery is single-path; the audit reports a false "absent."** `audit_harness.py:38` and `validate_harness.py:402` both hardcode `root / "CLAUDE.md"`.
